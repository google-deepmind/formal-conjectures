## Source-faithfulness statement and the one departure

The proof transcribed is Pastras §1 (1.31)–(1.32) (inversion of the elliptic integral by the substitution
`x = ℘(t)`), §3.1 (3.1) and Figure 3 (monotonicity of ℘ on the real axis between the pole and the real
half-period), Appendix A (A.1) (the real half-period is the first point after 0 where ℘' vanishes), with
the statement itself DLMF 23.6.34/23.6.36. Every leaf of the tree is one of the source's own steps, except
one: the source's justification that **℘' has no zeros other than the half-periods** is "℘' is an elliptic
function of order 3 and therefore it has exactly three roots in each cell" (Pastras p. 13; Silverman VI
Prop. 3.6(a) argues the same way). That count is the argument principle for elliptic functions, which is
**not in Mathlib** (red flag 4 of the source-faithfulness rule). Cross-referencing found no textbook proof
avoiding it. The leaf statement (`two_mul_mem_lattice_of_derivWeierstrassP_eq_zero`) is kept exactly as the
source's; its *proof* is replaced by a sub-tree (H8–H10) that formalises the "general solution"
argument the same source uses two pages earlier: Pastras p. 12 derives (1.32) by noting that the solutions
of the Weierstrass differential equation are `y = ℘(±z + z₀)`. Rigorously: `t ↦ ℘(z₀ + t)` and
`t ↦ ℘(z₀ − t)` solve the same second-order equation `u'' = 6u² − g₂/2` with the same initial data when
`℘'(z₀) = 0`, so they agree (Mathlib `ODE_solution_unique_of_eventually`), hence `2z₀` is a period, hence
`2z₀ ∈ Λ` by comparing orders at the pole `0`. Every leaf of that sub-tree is discharged from Mathlib or
project code. This is the only place where a Lean proof does not follow the cited sentence of the source;
it is flagged again at H8–H10 below.

### Plain-English proof of R1 (Step 1, mirroring Pastras §1, §3.1, App. A and DLMF 23.6)

Let `E/ℝ` be elliptic with two-torsion cubic `F(x) = 4x³ + b₂x² + 2b₄x + b₆`, largest real root `e₁`
(`WeierstrassCurve.e₁`). The substitution `x = X − b₂/12` turns `F` into the depressed cubic
`Q(X) = 4X³ − g₂X − g₃` with `g₂ = c₄/12`, `g₃ = c₆/216` (project lemma
`eval_toPoly_twoTorsionPolynomial_sub`), so `leastRealPeriodIntegral = 2 ∫_{e₁ + b₂/12}^∞ dX/√Q(X)`
(project lemma `leastRealPeriodIntegral_eq_integral_depressed`). Let `Λ` be the period lattice of `E`,
i.e. the lattice with invariants `g₂, g₃` (`WeierstrassCurve.periodPair`; F1, F2 below). Since `g₂, g₃`
are real, `Λ` is real (`periodPair_map_isReal`), and DLMF 23.5(i): "The Weierstrass functions take real
values on the real axis iff the lattice is fixed under complex conjugation" (R1–R7 below). Let `Ω` be
the least positive real period (`PeriodPair.leastRealPeriod`, specified by `isLeast_leastRealPeriod`).

*Behaviour of ℘ on `(0, Ω/2]`* (Pastras p. 30: "as one approaches a pole from the real axis, ℘ tends to
+∞ … as the derivative vanishes only at the half periods, ℘ is monotonous at the segments between
consequent half-periods and poles"): `℘(t) → +∞` as `t → 0⁺` (R11); `℘'(Ω/2) = 0` because `Ω/2` is a
half-period (R14, Pastras (1.35)); `℘'(t) ≠ 0` for `0 < t < Ω/2` because the zeros of `℘'` are the
half-periods (H11) and `2t ∈ (0, Ω)` is not a period (R13, R15; Pastras App. A: "there is no other such
position on the real axis between 0 and x"); `℘' < 0` there since `℘'(t) ~ −2t⁻³` near `0⁺` (R12, R16);
hence `℘` is strictly decreasing on `(0, Ω/2]` (R17) and maps `(0, Ω/2)` onto `(e₁', ∞)` where
`e₁' := ℘(Ω/2)` (R18).

*`e₁'` is the largest real root of `Q`* (DLMF 23.3.9–23.3.11, Pastras (1.36)): `Q(℘(Ω/2)) = ℘'(Ω/2)² = 0`
(R19); a real root `x > e₁'` would be `℘(t)` for some `t ∈ (0, Ω/2)`, where `℘'(t)² = Q(x) = 0`,
contradicting `℘'(t) ≠ 0` (R20). Therefore `e₁ + b₂/12 = e₁'` (F3).

*The integral* (Pastras (1.31)–(1.32), DLMF 23.6.34/36): substitute `X = ℘(t)`, `t ∈ (0, Ω/2)`:
`dX = ℘'(t) dt` and `√Q(℘(t)) = √(℘'(t)²) = |℘'(t)|`, so
`∫_{e₁'}^∞ dX/√Q(X) = ∫_0^{Ω/2} |℘'(t)| / |℘'(t)| dt = Ω/2` (R21, via Mathlib's one-dimensional change of
variables `integral_image_eq_integral_abs_deriv_smul`). Hence `leastRealPeriodIntegral = 2·(Ω/2) = Ω`.
